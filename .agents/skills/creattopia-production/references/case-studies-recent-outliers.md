# Case Studies: Recent Outlier Channels (data-backed)

Three live channels pulled via YouTube analytics (subscriber counts, outlier scores, and average views as of query time — these numbers move, treat them as a snapshot, not a permanent ranking). Unlike the mega-channel paradigms in `faceless-channels.md`, these are small/young channels caught mid-breakout, which makes them useful for a different question: **what does an outlier hit actually look like in the numbers, right as it's happening.**

## Zenn (@Zenn0009) — the "Big Question" stick-figure channel

162K subscribers, 22 videos, 16.4M total views, ~746K average views/video. Classified faceless (98% confidence), category science/history/philosophy/society, format: animation. This channel **is** the wobbly-MS-Paint stick-figure aesthetic — it's a live, currently-viral example of the exact style documented in `wobbly-image-sequence-prompt.md` and `faceless-channels.md`'s Casually Explained paradigm, applied to "big question" science/history content instead of comedy.

| Video | Views | Outlier score |
|---|---|---|
| "What Did Ancient Humans Do at Night?" | 7.7M | 10.3x |
| "The Calhoun Effect" | 3.8M | 5.1x |
| "Why You Can't Remember Being a Baby" | 1.8M | 2.4x |

**Title formula**: `[question word] + [ancient/natural/psychological subject] + [do/experience/happen]?` — poses a question the viewer didn't know they wanted answered, about something universally relatable (being a baby, night-time, memory) rather than niche trivia. All three run 7–9 minutes, consistent with a single-topic deep-dive rather than a quick-hit short.

## Axen (@AxenCool) — a same-format clone, days behind

41.4K subscribers, 11 videos, joined **April 2026** — a brand-new channel that reached its outlier hits within its first two months. Also faceless (98%), category history/education/science, format: animation. Keywords include "psychology," "overthinking," "ancient humans" — near-identical targeting to Zenn.

| Video | Views | Outlier score |
|---|---|---|
| "What Did Ancient Humans Do all Day Before Jobs Existed?" | 2.8M | 6.9x |
| "How Did Ancient Humans Survive the World's Deadliest Predators?" | 872K | 2.1x |

**The insight isn't the format — it's the speed of replication.** Axen's top title ("What Did Ancient Humans Do...") is a near-exact rerun of Zenn's top title, published within roughly a month of each other, in the same visual style. This is direct evidence for the growth-scripting playbook's implicit warning: once a hook format proves out, it gets cloned almost immediately by other channels running the same aesthetic. Two implications for planning:

1. Don't assume a validated hook format buys long exclusivity — expect competitors within weeks, not months.
2. Icon Factors and a distinct mission (see `vision-ikigai-icon.md`) are what survive format-cloning; the raw hook/aesthetic combination alone does not.

## NZTV Official (@NZTVOfficial) — real-footage wildlife documentary, not MS Paint

11.9K subscribers, 14 videos, joined April 2026, ~466K average views. Faceless (98%) but via a completely different mechanism than Zenn/Axen: real wildlife stock/archival footage with narration, no drawn assets and no on-camera host — "faceless" here means no presenter, not a crude-drawing style. Category: wildlife documentary.

| Video | Views | Outlier score |
|---|---|---|
| "Crocodile Attacks Shoebill Nest While It Guards Its Eggs… Here's What Happened!" | 2.5M | 5.4x |
| "A Wild Dog Tried to Steal Cassowary Eggs… Then This Happened \| From Egg to Adult" | 1.1M | 2.4x |

**Title formula**: `[predator] + [attacks/tries to steal] + [prey's nest/eggs] + cliffhanger ("...Then This Happened!")`. Structures every video as a life-or-death conflict with a withheld resolution, which is the wildlife-documentary equivalent of the Surreal E-Commerce payoff structure in `surreal-ecommerce.md` — open on stakes, withhold the outcome, deliver payoff at the end.

**Series framing**: "From Egg to Adult" is an explicit life-cycle series label stamped onto individual videos, which functions exactly like the content-series structure in `growth-scripting.md` — it turns a one-off conflict video into an entry point for a longer, groupable series (egg → hatchling → juvenile → adult) without requiring the viewer to have seen prior entries to understand this one.

## Takeaways to apply

1. **Faceless does not mean one aesthetic.** Zenn/Axen (drawn) and NZTV (real footage) are both 98%-confidence faceless channels in completely different visual paradigms — pick the paradigm based on niche and asset availability (see `faceless-channels.md`), not because "faceless" implies MS Paint.
2. **Conflict-and-withheld-payoff titles outperform description titles** across both paradigms here — "Crocodile Attacks Shoebill Nest... Here's What Happened!" and "What Did Ancient Humans Do at Night?" both work by opening a question/stakes the thumbnail+title don't answer.
3. **A single outlier can carry a whole channel's average.** Zenn's 746K average view count is dominated by one 7.7M-view breakout; don't mistake a channel's average for its typical performance — check the outlier spread, not just the mean, when evaluating whether a format is repeatable.
4. **New channels can reach 6–10x outlier multiples within 1–2 months** of launch when the format is already proven elsewhere (Axen, NZTV) — this is a realistic timeline expectation to set, not an outlier case for planning purposes.
