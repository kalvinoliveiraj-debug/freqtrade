# Strategic Blueprints for Sustainable Channel Growth

Move from unstructured daily posting to strategic, structured production: content series, a scripting formula matched to available time/budget, and a disciplined scaling order.

## Planning a content series

A series is 5–7 episodes on a single broad theme, taking the viewer through one shared journey/mission rather than publishing isolated, unrelated videos. Structured series build "true fans" — an audience that returns episode to episode — better than one-off hits do.

Before launching a series, answer a planning questionnaire (the source names this step without preserving the exact question list; use this as the working set):

1. What single theme/mission ties every episode together, and can it honestly sustain 5–7 distinct episodes without repeating itself?
2. What emotional arc should the *viewer* go through across the series (curiosity → understanding → conviction → action, or similar)?
3. Which Dream Avatar micro/macro challenge pair does each individual episode address (see `aaa-vva-dream-avatar.md`) — map one per episode so the series doesn't just restate the same point seven times?
4. What's the intended cadence (daily/weekly) and does the production pipeline (see `production-blueprint.md`) actually support that cadence at the chosen scripting formula (below)?
5. What's the intended final episode's payoff/CTA, and does every prior episode build toward it?

## The four-tier scripting system

Pick a formula based on time constraints and camera comfort — don't default to the most detailed option out of habit; over-scripting a comfortable, spontaneous creator wastes pre-production time, and under-scripting an uncomfortable one produces a weak take.

| Formula | Pre-production demand | Screenplay complexity | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The Tryhard Method | Word-for-word scripting of all dialogue. | Highly detailed shot-by-shot checklist. | High-budget productions, or creators with significant time to invest per video. |
| 2. The Efficient Method | Word-for-word hook, then a mixed script of bullet points and key lines. | Optional shot checklist; leans on spontaneous B-roll. | The average creator balancing quality and volume — recommended default. |
| 3. The Dot-Point Method | Standard bullet points only; dialogue is conversational/improvised. | Minimal descriptions; relies on existing stock/asset libraries. | Camera-comfortable creators with natural, fluid on-camera delivery. |
| 4. The Brazy Method | No scripted dialogue at all. | No screenplay or visual planning; just a strong core hook. | Testing highly unconventional or experimental concepts. |

## Hormozi's "More, Better, New" scaling model

Apply Alex Hormozi's growth-prioritization principle to content production, in strict order — don't skip ahead to "new" formats before the current format has been maximized:

1. **More** — before changing anything, simply produce more of the content/format that is already working (more episodes, higher frequency) to confirm the ceiling hasn't been hit yet.
2. **Better** — once volume is maxed for the current resources, improve the existing format's quality/efficiency (tighter scripts, stronger hooks, better pacing/editing) rather than diversifying.
3. **New** — only once More and Better are exhausted, introduce new formats, series, or platforms. Expanding into "new" too early is the most common way creators dilute a format that hadn't actually plateaued yet.

Use this as the gate before greenlighting a brand-new series or format: can the current one still get More or Better first?
